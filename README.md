# AI and Social Media Usage in Research and its Impact on Traditional Search Engines

## intro

In the modern digital era, the landscape of academic and general research has undergone a profound transformation. For decades, traditional search engines—most notably Google—served as the primary gateways to information. However, the rapid emergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) platforms and the evolution of social media platforms into unconventional search engines have fundamentally altered how individuals discover, gather, and assess information. Today, researchers navigate a complex ecosystem of competing tools, each offering distinct advantages.

While traditional search engines index web pages based on algorithms, AI tools synthesize immediate answers, and social media platforms offer highly visual, community-driven knowledge. This shifting dynamic raises significant questions regarding user satisfaction, perceived data security, trust, and information verification habits. Understanding these preferences sheds light on whether traditional search frameworks are being systematically outpaced or if they remain crucial backbones of information security.

## prepare and analyze

The data for this study was collected via a comprehensive 30-variable consumer questionnaire. The dataset consists of responses from a sample composed of 57.89% Male and 42.11% Female participants, leaning primarily toward university-level students.

* Active Research Survey: [QuestionPro Survey Link](https://hassanibrahimelsayaf.questionpro.com/t/AdE2PZ8vgT)
* Survey Document Reference: [View Survey Layout on pdfFiller](https://www.pdffiller.com/s/m9BtmrQKLJ)

*Core Tool Usage Metrics*
* **SocialMedia_usage (75.4%)**: Heavily utilize social media platforms as a utility when searching.
* **AI_usage (73.6%)**: Heavily rely on Artificial Intelligence tools ("a lot") for conducting research.
* **SearchEngine_usage (68.4%)**: Traditional search engines (like Google) recorded the lowest heavy-usage percentage.

*Platform Preference Drivers*
* **Information Quantity (54.3%)**: Absolute volume and abundance of data resources is the dominant choice factor.
* **Search Speed (49.1%)**: Speed of obtaining quick information drives platform selection.
* **Information Trust (29.8%)**: Perceived data accuracy dictates primary tool selection for less than a third of users.
* **Data Security (17.5%)**: Security is a minor priority driver.


## conclusion

By synthesizing our descriptive data and statistical distributions, here are the answers to our core research questions:

**1- How does the frequency of AI usage compare to search engines and social media when conducting research?**
Paradoxically, traditional search engines have the lowest heavy-usage percentage (68.4%) among the three mediums. Social media leads with a 75.4% heavy-usage frequency, followed closely by AI utilities at 73.6%. However, when users rank their strict daily priorities, AI tools (40.3%) and Search Engines (38.6%) decisively secure the top functional rank, while social media is actively pushed to a last-choice routine tool by 54.4% of the population.

**2- What are the primary factors that drive user preference for specific research tools?**
User preference is overwhelmingly driven by an abundance/quantity of information (54.3%) and the operational speed of acquiring data (49.1%). Conversely, structural integrity factors like Information Trust (29.8%) and Data Security (17.5%) serve as minor secondary priority metrics.

**3- What are the overall satisfaction levels for AI, Search Engines, and Social Media?**
Both Search Engines (Mean: 69.56) and AI tools (Mean: 64.17) display high overall satisfaction, showing a strong population concentration toward elevated scores. Social Media satisfaction diverges with a significantly lower mean (42.40) and a flattened distribution (Platykurtic), reflecting a wide variance in user sentiment and less uniform consensus compared to the dedicated research tools.

**4- Is the proportion of individuals who completely avoid using AI, Search Engines, or Social Media significantly different from an expected 20% baseline?**
The data establishes that 22.8% of respondents explicitly avoid using social media channels when looking for serious research data. This observed baseline rests remarkably close to the hypothesized 20% avoidance threshold, confirming that a stable fifth of the research demographic systematically isolates social media away from their serious information pipelines.

**5- To what extent do users verify the information they gather during research, and what verification methods do they rely on?**
Fact verification is an incredibly consistent habit within the demographic. Users heavily depend on human-centric verification vectors, showing a major preference for written reviews combined with quantitative ratings, alongside an explicit validation requirement for real human perspectives to confirm the legitimacy of retrieved facts.

**6- When social media is utilized for research, which specific platforms are the most dominant?**
YouTube dominates social media research applications, heavily relied upon by 59.6% of the surveyed population. Facebook holds the secondary position with an active utilization rate of 42.1%. Newer short-form channels or specialized networks fail to achieve a dominant research foothold (TikTok 24.6%, Instagram 15.8%, Reddit 14.0%, X/Twitter 7.0%).
